\section{Binary Notifications} 
Not only is it trivial to encode data about a notificiation in a binary format but it's also trivial to imagine a phones vibration as a bit of information, based on one of its properties (\eg a short vibration as a zero and a long vibration as a one); all in all it's rather trivial to encode data about a notification in its corrosponding vibration.

I have done exactly this, by programming a simple Android app which listens to notifications, understands their content (based on app, and on its title), and vibrates in a specific pattern accordingly.

\subsection{Values}
While making sure that I know everything about all of my notifications as soon as I receive them does go against many of the values I strive for, mainly that of being less distracted by my phone (and other technology), I seem to have done it anyway.

One can however also easily argue it will scratch the itch of wanting to pull out the phone to immediately know where a notification came from, which would usually followed by not putting the phone back down for a longer than necesarry.

It is to be seen whether this will succeed, be useful, or be thought of as a stupid idea in 1, or 3, or 6 months time.

\emph{Maybe Important} may mean notifications whose importance can't entirely be determined (\eg claims to be rather important, but can't be found in the above list), or notifications from other non-social-media instant messaging apps (the SMS app, WhatsApp, or Signal).

Other notifications should simply not vibrate.

Any of these notifications or categories of notifications might be muted, for example; while at work, at school, or trying to study or focus, it's probably beneficial to mute online friends.
