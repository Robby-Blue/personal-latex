\section{Time zones}
It's not always the same time everywhere on earth, because it's round; so time zones exist.

Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)[a] is the primary time standard globally used to regulate clocks and time


\subsection{The Greenwich Mean Time}
To really express what time it is in a given time zone, it has to be compared to another standardised timezone with a standardised meridian. This is what GMT was used for.

This is the Greenwich Mean Time (GMT), based on the Greenwich Meridian, as measured from the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London.

\subsection{UTC}
Nowadays the primary standard used to regulate clocks and time is UTC (Coordinated Universal Time). Timezones can be offset from UTC by a specific amonut of hours \texttt{HH} and minutes \texttt{MM}, either ahead or behind. They're described as being \texttt{UTC+HH:MM} (or \texttt{UTC-HH:MM}) or also just being \texttt{+HH:MM} (or \texttt{-HH:MM}).

\texttt{UTC+00:00} is GMT.
